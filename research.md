# Research: ISO 24495-1 operationalization (2024–2026)

## Summary

ISO 24495-1:2023 is a **guidance/principles** standard, not a certifiable management system. Official operationalization in 2024–2026 is happening through IPLF supporting materials and case studies, national adoptions (Australia identical; UK/US sell-only), and Canada’s free, more detailed **CAN-ASC-3.1:2025**, which aligns with ISO 24495-1 in places and differs because it is an accessibility standard. Public agent tooling exists, but every ISO-named skill is unofficial; the safest prior art is IPLF’s four reader-outcome principles plus rewrite/review skills that refuse conformance claims.

## Findings

1. **Official nature of the standard** — ISO 24495-1:2023 *Plain language — Part 1: Governing principles and guidelines* “establishes governing principles and guidelines for developing plain language documents.” IPLF states the four principles as: readers get what they need (**relevant**), can find it (**findable**), can understand it (**understandable**), and can use it (**usable**). The IPLF/PLAIN definition is outcome-based: wording, structure, and design so clear that intended readers can find, understand, and use the information. IPLF says Part 1 is language-neutral, drafted by experts from 25 countries / 19 languages, and that only about 7% of its ideas concern word choice. [IPLF ISO page](https://www.iplfederation.org/iso-standard/) · [PLAIN](https://plainlanguage.com/what-is-plain-language/iso-plain-language-standard/) · [ISO catalog](https://www.iso.org/standard/78907.html)

2. **Not a certifiable management system today** — IPLF is explicit: “The current standard is for guidance only and is not for certification.” A Certification Committee consultation paper (June 2024) explores future organizational certification. ISO is developing **ISO 24495-4**, *Requirements for implementing the plain language principles in organizations*, so an organization could later certify that it uses plain language systematically. Search catalogs list this as ISO/DIS 24495-4 (`90061`); IPLF still describes it as in development. Commercial pages that sell “ISO 24495-1 certification” today are not an official ISO scheme. [IPLF](https://www.iplfederation.org/iso-standard/) · [consultation PDF](https://www.iplfederation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Plain-language-certification-for-organizations_A-Consultation-Paper-1-1.pdf) · [ISO/DIS 24495-4](https://www.iso.org/standard/90061.html)

3. **Official implementation help is IPLF, not a free ISO checklist** — IPLF publishes (or indexes) supporting documents, testimonials, case studies, an adopting guide, and an adopting checklist. Supporting documents are **not ISO publications**. Indexed items include a healthcare implementation guide (*Clear Communication for Better Health*, revised 2024/2025), a forthcoming document-design Pattern Library for unpublished Part 5, a research bibliography, and case studies from the Australian Taxation Office, Australian health brochures, Canadian Centre on Substance Use and Addiction alcohol guidance, and a 2026 “From Academia to Practice” Canadian case. The IPLF **adopting checklist** is for getting a national body to adopt the standard, not for scoring a document. The `/supporting-documents/` landing page 404’d on fetch even though search still indexes it. [IPLF ISO page](https://www.iplfederation.org/iso-standard/) · [adopting checklist](https://www.iplfederation.org/adopting-checklist) · [healthcare PDF](https://www.iplfederation.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Clear-Communication-for-Better-Health.pdf)

4. **National-body status (Australia, Canada, EU, UK, US)**
   - **Australia:** adopted identically as **AS ISO 24495-1:2023 / AS ISO 24495.1:2024**. IPLF published an ATO website-tips case study as an implementation example. Practitioner commentary treats it as a citable governance lever beside WCAG and the Style Manual (unofficial). [IPLF translations](https://www.iplfederation.org/standard-translations/) · [ATO case study](https://www.iplfederation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ISO-Case-Study_Australian-Taxation-Office.pdf)
   - **Canada:** SCC/ISO channel is **sell-only** for ISO 24495-1. Separately, Accessibility Standards Canada published free **CAN-ASC-3.1:2025 – Plain Language** (13 Oct 2025). Official preface: it is “intended to align with” ISO 24495-1 and EN 301 549, “aligns … in many ways and differs in others,” is voluntary unless later regulated under the Accessible Canada Act, and **“can be used for conformity assessment.”** It is longer (~172 pp vs ~22), accessibility-barrier-focused, multilingual (EN/FR plus sign languages), and names Easy Read as an option for memory/attention barriers. Desjardins is an early adopter for a digital content guide. [ASC standard](https://accessible.canada.ca/creating-accessibility-standards/can-asc-312025-plain-language) · [preface](https://accessible.canada.ca/creating-accessibility-standards/can-asc-312025-plain-language/5-preface) · [Center for Plain Language summary](https://centerforplainlanguage.org/excitement-grows-about-canadas-new-accessible-plain-language-standard/)
   - **EU:** AccessibleEU catalogs ISO 24495-1 as a digital-library item. No EU-wide ISO 24495 checklist was found. Easy Language / Inclusion Europe easy-to-read is a **different** register. [AccessibleEU](https://accessible-eu-centre.ec.europa.eu/content-corner/digital-library/iso-24495-12023-plain-language-part-1-governing-principles-and-guidelines_en) · [Inclusion Europe](https://easy-to-read.inclusion-europe.eu/european-standards/)
   - **UK:** BSI **sells** BS ISO 24495-1:2023; not listed as adopted. GOV.UK still runs its own mandatory plain-English style guidance, with no ISO 24495 citation found. [IPLF translations](https://www.iplfederation.org/standard-translations/) · [GOV.UK clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)
   - **US:** ANSI/ISO channel is **sell-only**. Operational US law remains the **Plain Writing Act of 2010** plus Federal Plain Language Guidelines / Digital.gov guides and agency checklists (e.g. Interior). Those are official US practice, **not ISO 24495 implementations**. [IPLF translations](https://www.iplfederation.org/standard-translations/) · [Digital.gov](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language) · [DOI](https://www.doi.gov/plainlanguage)
   - Other identical adoptions listed by IPLF include AT, BE, BR, CZ, DK, FI, FR, DE, IE, IT, NO, PT, SK, ZA, ES, SE. [IPLF translations](https://www.iplfederation.org/standard-translations/)

6. **Agent skills and GitHub prior art** (catalog survey; unofficial unless noted)

   **ISO-named**
   - [danyuchn/iso-24495-skill](https://github.com/danyuchn/iso-24495-skill) — MIT (Dustin Yuchen Teng, 2026). **Rewrite** skill for Claude Code (also described as Agent Skills-compatible). Shared four-principle layer + English and Traditional Chinese technique files. Default output is rewritten text only; optional principle-tagged diff. Explicitly unofficial; does not reproduce ISO text; refuses to drop facts; flags precision trade-offs; points machine-facing text to ASD-STE100.
   - [GaZmagik/iso-24495](https://github.com/GaZmagik/iso-24495) — MIT (Gareth Williams, 2026). Five skills + Claude plugin + output style + Bun markdown linter/hook + Part 4 maturity audit. Modes: **always-on rewrite** (Part 1), legal (2), science/tech (3), **org gap analysis** (4, provisional), **document design** (5, provisional). Strongest engineering prior art, but examples literally label output “ISO 24495-1 Aligned” even while the README forbids conformance claims. Hard proxies (max 20 words/sentence) are the author’s, not ISO’s. Parts 4–5 are built from public scopes of unpublished drafts.

   **Related, not ISO**
   - [JoshuaGoode/plain-writing](https://github.com/JoshuaGoode/plain-writing) — MIT. Codex + Claude plugin. Draft / edit / **audit** + deterministic `lint_prose.py`. Protects facts, hedges, and voice. Influenced by Orwell / STE goals; **not** ISO. Clean lint ≠ quality.
   - [SpiritWrestlerSolutions/luddite-lookover](https://github.com/SpiritWrestlerSolutions/luddite-lookover) — MIT. **Review-first** skill. Requires an audience contract; jargon is relative to the reader. Suggests fixes rather than rewriting by default.
   - [shreyashankar/plain-writing-skill](https://github.com/shreyashankar/plain-writing-skill) (also `docwriter-org/plain-writing-skill`) — MIT. Style rules + `/plain-writing deslopify` rewrite + evals. Not ISO.
   - Adjacent de-slop linters (`ozdemircili/deslop`, `shessenauer/deslop-ai-lint-skill`, `SamGalanakis/pilcrow`, `forjd/better-writing`) optimize AI-tell removal or classical style, not ISO reader outcomes.

7. **How organizations evaluate without buying certification** — ISO/IPLF treat success as **reader outcome**, not a grade level. Practical non-cert methods in official or practitioner sources:
   - Identify intended readers, purpose, and context, then expert-review against the four principles.
   - Reader/user testing (IPLF and Plain Numbers both treat **usable** as requiring research/testing, i.e. service design, not only editing).
   - Criteria-based assessment / structured checklists (Frances Gordon and others contrast this with readability scores).
   - For Canada, use CAN-ASC-3.1 itself for conformity assessment while it remains voluntary.
   - IPLF case studies as qualitative implementation evidence, not certificates.
   **Flesch / Flesch–Kincaid / Hemingway / SMOG / Fog** measure syllables and sentence length. They do not test findability, relevance, actionability, design, or whether the *intended* reader can use the document. UX and plain-language literature has long advised against using them as pass/fail (e.g. [UXmatters](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2019/07/readability-formulas-7-reasons-to-avoid-them-and-what-to-do-instead.php)). Hemingway is a Flesch–Kincaid UI, not an ISO tool. [Plain Numbers](https://plainnumbers.org.uk/news-insights/find-it-understand-it-use-it-the-iso-standard-redefines-plain-language-as-a-user-outcome/)

8. **Adjacent standards**
   - **ISO 24495-2:2025** *Legal communication* — published August 2025. Applies Part 1 in legal contexts: multiple audiences, legally required structure, complex concepts, processes for exercising rights/obligations. IPLF: plain legal language can be as precise as traditional legalese and often exposes hidden ambiguity. [IPLF Part 2 release](https://www.iplfederation.org/press-release-part-2/) · [ISO 85774](https://www.iso.org/standard/85774.html)
   - **ISO 24495-3:2026** *Science writing* — IPLF says published May 2026. Science writing for mixed-expertise readers, **not** primarily expert-to-expert journal articles. [IPLF](https://www.iplfederation.org/iso-standard/) · [ISO 86938](https://www.iso.org/standard/86938.html)
   - **ISO 24495-4** (in development) — organizational requirements; intended future certification vehicle. [ISO 90061](https://www.iso.org/standard/90061.html)
   - **ISO 24495-5** document design — unpublished working draft; IPLF Pattern Library planned.
   - **ISO 24896:2026** *Notation for business reporting* — same WG, not part of 24495; visual notation for charts/tables.
   - **Easy Language / Easy-to-Read** — Inclusion Europe European standards and national Easy Language guidelines target people with intellectual/reading disabilities with much tighter constraints. CAN-ASC treats Easy Read as a specialist option, not a synonym for plain language. [Inclusion Europe](https://easy-to-read.inclusion-europe.eu/european-standards/)
   - **WCAG 2.x SC 3.1.5 Reading Level (AAA)** — if text needs more than lower-secondary reading ability (after removing proper names/titles), provide a simpler version or supplement. Complementary accessibility criterion; not ISO 24495 and not WCAG A/AA. [W3C Understanding 3.1.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reading-level.html)
   - **EN 301 549 / CAN-ASC EN 301 549** — ICT accessibility; CAN-ASC-3.1 explicitly aligns with it.
   - **ASD-STE100** — controlled language for zero-ambiguity technical English; complementary to, not the same as, ISO 24495 (human reader vs restricted grammar).

9. **Risks of a naive agent implementation**
   - **False “ISO compliant” claims.** Part 1 cannot be certified. Skills that stamp “ISO 24495-1 Aligned” (GaZmagik examples) create legal/reputational risk even with a README disclaimer.
   - **Readability-score worship.** 20-word sentence caps and Flesch targets can punish necessary legal/scientific terms and ignore findability/usability.
   - **Oversimplification.** Part 2 exists because legal precision must survive plainness. A rewrite that drops conditions, hedges, actors, or rights is a compliance failure, not a win.
   - **English-only assumptions.** The standard is language-neutral; techniques do not translate. US Plain Writing Act practice is English-centric. Chinese/other technique layers must be original, not calques.
   - **Collapsing Easy Language into plain language.** Over-simplifying for a general adult audience can insult specialists or erase legally required detail; under-simplifying fails readers with cognitive disabilities.
   - **Reproducing ISO text.** The standard is sold (ISO lists ~CHF pricing). Skills must use public IPLF principles, not leaked clauses.
   - **Treating unpublished Parts 4–5 as law.** Maturity models built from committee-draft scopes will drift when ISO publishes.
   - **Always-on rewrite of code, logs, or agent-facing specs.** GaZmagik correctly exempts code; danyuchn correctly routes machine readers to STE.

## What an agent skill can safely do vs must not claim

**Can safely do**
- Ask who the intended reader is and what they must do.
- Review or rewrite against the **public** four outcomes (relevant / findable / understandable / usable).
- Flag jargon relative to that reader; protect facts, numbers, conditions, negations, and honest uncertainty.
- Separate review mode from rewrite mode; show principle-tagged diffs.
- Recommend human expert review and reader tests; treat automated metrics as weak signals only.
- Point legal/science/org-design work to Parts 2/3/4/5 as *published or draft*, with status labels.
- State that the skill is unofficial and that the full ISO text was not reproduced.

**Must not claim**
- “ISO 24495-1 compliant,” “certified,” or “meets the standard.”
- That a Flesch/Hemingway/word-count pass equals ISO.
- That unpublished Part 4/5 skills implement the ISO draft text.
- That Easy Language, WCAG 3.1.5, or the US Plain Writing Act is ISO 24495.
- That the skill inspected or is a substitute for the purchased ISO document.

## Best existing prior art to adapt

1. **Normative public layer:** IPLF four principles + case-study process (official-adjacent, no ISO text). Pair with purchased ISO only if a human must do formal interpretation.
2. **Free detailed government layer (if accessibility/federal-Canada is in scope):** CAN-ASC-3.1:2025 — only official **conformity-assessable** plain-language standard found that is free and ISO-aware.
3. **Best rewrite skill to steal structure from:** `danyuchn/iso-24495-skill` — principle/technique split, bilingual proof that techniques are language-specific, fidelity rule, honest copyright note.
4. **Best review/audience prior art:** `luddite-lookover` — audience contract; review not rewrite.
5. **Best engineering/linter prior art, use with caution:** `GaZmagik/iso-24495` — plugin, hook, corpus audit, maturity model. Strip “Aligned/MUST follow ISO” language and all unpublished-part overclaim. Keep exemptions for thinking/code.
6. **Best non-ISO prose linter:** `JoshuaGoode/plain-writing` — deterministic lint + protected tokens; do not rebrand as ISO.

Do **not** start from de-slop skills if the goal is ISO-style reader outcomes. They optimize accent, not find/understand/use.
