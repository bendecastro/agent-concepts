# LLM self-preference, self-correction, and sycophancy — evidence for agent code review

**Question:** A builder agent writes code; a reviewer agent is spawned **fresh** (no shared conversation, given diff/spec). Does it matter *materially* if the reviewer is the same model family/provider as the builder vs a different provider?

**Hypothesis under test:** Same-family judges are lenient / miss bugs because they recognize or prefer “their own” style; intrinsic self-critique is weak; seeing the builder’s rationale makes the reviewer sycophantic.

**How to read this note:** Each item is tagged `PEER-REVIEWED` / `PREPRINT` / `BLOG-VENDOR` / `ANECDOTE`. **Evidence** = what the paper measured. **Inference** = this note’s interpretation for the owner’s setup. Evidence that *cuts against* same-family-is-dangerous is marked **AGAINST**.

**Model-era caveat:** Core self-preference and self-correction papers are GPT-3.5/4, Claude-1/2, Llama-2 era (2023–2024). Chen et al. 2025 and Jin & Chen 2026 use later models (Llama-3.x, Qwen2.5, GPT-4o, Gemma-2). Transfer to 2025–2026 coding agents is uncertain.

**Validation note:** Automated `source_check` did not produce a semantic support/contradiction score (tool returned “unclear”). Numbers below are taken from fetched HTML of the papers themselves (arxiv HTML / NeurIPS / ICLR / TACL).

## 1. Self-preference / self-recognition in LLM-as-judge

### 1.1 Zheng et al. — first “self-enhancement” flag (inconclusive)

- **Source:** Lianmin Zheng, Wei-Lin Chiang, Ying Sheng, Siyuan Zhuang, Zhanghao Wu, Yonghao Zhuang, Zi Lin, Zhuohan Li, Dacheng Li, Eric P. Xing, Hao Zhang, Joseph E. Gonzalez, Ion Stoica. “Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena.” NeurIPS 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685  
- **Status:** PEER-REVIEWED  
- **Measured:** Pairwise LLM-as-judge vs human votes on MT-Bench / Arena-style chat. Win rates of six models under GPT-4, Claude-v1, GPT-3.5 vs humans.  
- **Numbers:** GPT-4’s self win-rate ~**10 percentage points** above human baseline; Claude-v1 ~**25 points** above. GPT-3.5 did **not** favor itself. Position bias was large (only GPT-4 consistent in >60% of swapped-order cases on similar answers). GPT-4–human agreement >80%.  
- **Setting:** Open-ended chat, **not code**. Models: GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Claude-v1 (2023). Judge sees answers, not a prior generation session.  
- **Evidence:** Authors **explicitly refuse to conclude** self-enhancement: “Due to limited data and small differences, our study cannot determine whether the models exhibit a self-enhancement bias.” Rephrasing to another model’s style would also change quality.  
- **AGAINST (partial):** This is the paper that popularized the worry; its own controlled claim is “cannot determine.”

### 1.2 Panickssery, Bowman, Feng — self-recognition correlates with self-preference

- **Source:** Arjun Panickssery, Samuel R. Bowman, Shi Feng. “LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations.” NeurIPS 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076  
- **Status:** PEER-REVIEWED  
- **Measured:** Summarization (CNN/DailyMail, XSUM; 1,000 articles each). Generators: Llama-2-7b-chat, GPT-3.5, GPT-4 (temp 0). Same model later prompted as evaluator (pairwise quality) and authorship identifier. **No conversational memory of generation** — texts are re-presented. Pairwise + individual (Likert / yes-no) measurements; order swapped. Fine-tune GPT-3.5 and Llama-2 to raise/lower self-recognition.  
- **Numbers:** Out of the box, all three >50% pairwise self-recognition vs other sources. **GPT-4 73.5% accurate** distinguishing itself from two other LLMs and humans. After fine-tuning on 500 examples, GPT-3.5 and Llama-2 >90% self-recognition. Self-preference strength is **linearly correlated** with self-recognition (their Fig. 1). GPT-4 most capable at recognition; Llama-2 cannot distinguish itself from GPT-3.5/4. Individual-setting GPT-4 is “extremely unwilling to predict any text as having been generated by itself,” including its own.  
- **Setting:** News **summarization, not code**. 2023-era GPT-3.5/4 and Llama-2-7B-chat.  
- **Evidence:** Same-weights judge prefers own summaries vs other LLMs/humans even when humans rate them equal (authors’ definition of self-preference). Fine-tuning experiments are offered as evidence that recognition **causally** contributes (they say the causal story “resists straightforward confounders”).  
- **Inference (not in paper):** A **fresh reviewer session does not remove this effect** — that *is* their experimental setup. The mechanism they favor is prosaic self-recognition (distinguish own text), not “remembering I wrote this.”

### 1.3 Wataoka, Takahashi, Ri — perplexity / familiarity, not labeled selfhood

- **Source:** Koki Wataoka, Tsubasa Takahashi, Ryokan Ri. “Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge.” ICLR 2025 (arxiv 2410.21819). https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819  
- **Status:** PEER-REVIEWED (ICLR 2025 proceedings PDF exists; originally arxiv Oct 2024).  
- **Measured:** Pairwise quality judgments on Chatbot Arena pairs (~33k dialogues with human labels). Eight judges: GPT-4, GPT-3.5-Turbo, Vicuna-7/13B, Koala-13B, oasst-pythia-12B, dolly-v2-12B, stablelm-tuned-alpha-7B. Metric: Equal-Opportunity gap  
  `Bias = P(judge prefers own | human prefers own) − P(judge prefers own | human prefers other)`.  
  Position swapped. Then, for open models, bin judgments by **perplexity difference**.  
- **Numbers:** **GPT-4 bias = 0.520** (recall when humans like GPT-4: **0.945** = 1852/(108+1852); recall when humans dislike GPT-4: **0.425** = 118/(160+118)). Vicuna-13B and Koala-13B also high; several smaller models near **zero or negative** (they *under*-rate themselves). Own generations have **lower mean log-perplexity** than others for every model. At matched perplexity, win rates for own vs other-model text are **not significantly different**. Judges overweight low-perplexity vs humans. GPT-4/3.5 excluded from PPL analysis (no logits).  
- **Setting:** Open-ended **dialogue, not code**. Mix of 2023 chat models + GPT-4. Judge is **not told** which response is its own. Fresh prompt.  
- **Evidence:** Self-preference can appear **without an authorship label**. Authors’ interpretation: bias is **familiarity / lower perplexity**, i.e. “texts I would have generated,” not explicit self-recognition. Directly challenges Panickssery’s mechanism.  
- **AGAINST (for “must switch provider”):** If the driver is perplexity/style familiarity, a **same-family sibling** (or any model with similar style prior) can show the same bias. Conversely, some models have **reverse** self-preference.

### 1.4 Chen et al. — much self-preference is legitimate; harmful remainder is worse in strong models

- **Source:** Wei-Lin Chen, Zhepei Wei, Xinyu Zhu, Shi Feng, Yu Meng. “Do LLM Evaluators Prefer Themselves for a Reason?” arXiv:2504.03846, 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03846  
- **Status:** PREPRINT (as of fetch; not treated as published here).  
- **Measured:** Pairwise judging on **verifiable** tasks so “own is better” can be scored: MATH500, MMLU, **MBPP+ code** (executable tests). Families: Llama, Qwen, Gemma, Mistral, Phi, GPT, DeepSeek-R1-distill. Same evaluatee pool for all judges. Splits: `D_diff` (exactly one of own/other correct) vs `D_agree`. Metrics: self-preference ratio (SPR), legitimate SPR (LSPR), harmful self-preference propensity (HSPP = prefer own **when own is wrong and other is right**). Also LMArena subjective set (12,848 samples).  
- **Numbers:** Judge accuracy vs generator accuracy Pearson **r = 0.795 / 0.708 / 0.899** on MATH500 / MMLU / **MBPP+**. SPR vs task acc **r = 0.801 / 0.817 / 0.771**. LSPR for Qwen2.5-70B and Llama-3-70B: **96.57% and 95.16%** on MATH500; **80.98–88.78%** on MBPP+ and MMLU. **AGAINST naive bias story:** stronger models prefer themselves *mostly because they are actually better*.  
  **Harmful remainder:** Qwen2.5-72B HSPP **86% MATH500, 73% MMLU**. On **MBPP+**, large models HSPP ~**50–75%** while overall SPR **<40%**. Long CoT / R1-distill **reduces** HSPP.  
- **Setting:** **Includes code generation judging** (MBPP+). 2024–2025 open models + GPT. Fresh pairwise prompts, no generation transcript.  
- **Evidence:** Two facts at once: (1) most same-model preference on code/math is **quality**, not vanity; (2) **when the generator is wrong**, stronger models are *worse* at admitting it (overconfidence).  
- **Inference:** For a reviewer judging a *peer’s* patch, “same family” is dangerous mainly on the **subset where the builder’s code is actually wrong** and looks like the reviewer’s own mistakes — the case you care about.

### 1.5 Fresh session?

**Evidence (direct):** Panickssery, Wataoka, Zheng, and Chen all score texts in a **new judging prompt** with no shared chat of the generation. Self-preference still appears.

**Inference:** Spawning a reviewer in a fresh session **does not** neutralize same-weights self-preference. It *does* neutralize **within-conversation anchoring** (see §3), which is a different bias.

## 2. Self-correction: intrinsic vs external feedback

Distinction used by Huang and Kamoi:

| | Intrinsic | External |
|---|---|---|
| Feedback source | Same LLM’s own critique / “think again” | Tests, interpreter, compiler, search, oracle labels, humans, other models |
| Typical result (reasoning) | Flat or **worse** | Often helps if the signal is reliable |

### 2.1 Huang et al. — intrinsic reasoning self-correction fails; oracle feedback is cheating

- **Source:** Jie Huang, Xinyun Chen, Swaroop Mishra, Huaixiu Steven Zheng, Adams Wei Yu, Xinying Song, Denny Zhou (UIUC + Google DeepMind). “Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet.” ICLR 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798  
- **Status:** PEER-REVIEWED  
- **Measured:** GSM8K, CommonSenseQA, HotpotQA. GPT-3.5-turbo-0613, GPT-4 (2023-08-29), GPT-4-Turbo-1106, Llama-2-70b-chat. Generate → critique → revise, max 2 rounds. With vs without **oracle labels** that stop correction once the answer is already right.  
- **Numbers:** With oracle labels, prior work’s gains replicate (Kim et al. ~**+7% GSM8K**, ~**+15% CommonSenseQA**). **Without oracle:** “accuracies of all models drop across all benchmarks” (tables not fully extracted as cells in HTML; qualitative result is uniform). On GSM8K, GPT-3.5 **keeps the initial answer 74.7%** of the time; among changes, **correct→incorrect > incorrect→correct**. Multi-agent debate (3 agents, 2 rounds) on full GSM8K: debate 9 responses **83.0%** vs self-consistency 9 responses **88.2%** (standard prompting 76.7%). Authors explicitly contrast this with **code**: Chen et al. Self-Debugging works because the **executor is a perfect verifier** when unit tests exist.  
- **Setting:** Math / QA reasoning, **not code review**. 2023 GPT-3.5/4 and Llama-2-70B.  
- **Evidence:** Intrinsic self-critique of reasoning is a **bad judge of its own correctness**. Same-model “review then revise” without tests can **inject errors**.  
- **AGAINST over-generalizing to code:** Paper itself says tool/test feedback is the case where correction *should* work.

### 2.2 Kamoi et al. — survey: prompted self-feedback does not generally work

- **Source:** Ryo Kamoi, Yusen Zhang, Nan Zhang, Jiawei Han, Rui Zhang. “When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes? A Critical Survey of Self-Correction of LLMs.” TACL 12:1417–1440, 2024. https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.78/  
- **Status:** PEER-REVIEWED  
- **Measured:** Critical survey of self-correction literature (reasoning, QA, **code generation**, etc.). Bottleneck = **feedback quality**.  
- **Numbers (claims, not a new experiment):** (1) **No prior work** shows reliable self-correction with *prompted LLM feedback* on general tasks, except tasks “exceptionally suited” (e.g. decomposable outputs). (2) Works well with **reliable external feedback**. (3) Large-scale **fine-tuning** can enable self-correction. Many papers over-claim via oracle labels, weak initial prompts, or unfair baselines.  
- **Setting:** Includes code papers (Self-Refine, CRITIC, Reflexion, Self-Debug). Mixed 2022–2024 models.  
- **Evidence:** Same-model reviewer without tests ≈ the setting Kamoi says **fails**. Reviewer *with* failing tests / linter / typechecker ≈ the setting that **works**.

### 2.3 CRITIC — “self-correct” that is actually tool-interactive

- **Source:** Zhibin Gou, Zhihong Shao, Yeyun Gong, Yelong Shen, Yujiu Yang, Nan Duan, Weizhu Chen (Tsinghua + Microsoft). “CRITIC: Large Language Models Can Self-Correct with Tool-Interactive Critiquing.” ICLR 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11738  
- **Status:** PEER-REVIEWED  
- **Measured:** Free-form QA, **mathematical program synthesis** (code interpreter), toxicity. LLM verifies via search / interpreter, then revises.  
- **Numbers:** Paper reports **consistent gains** with tools; HTML tables were not fully extracted here. Authors stress **external feedback is crucial**; exclusive reliance on self-critique is unreliable.  
- **Setting:** Includes **code interpreter** for math programs. GPT-era + LLaMA-2.  
- **Evidence:** The successful “self-correction” brand is **not intrinsic**. It is generate → **run tools** → revise.

### 2.4 Self-Refine — same-model feedback, mostly *style/quality* not correctness

- **Source:** Aman Madaan et al. “Self-Refine: Iterative Refinement with Self-Feedback.” NeurIPS 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651  
- **Status:** PEER-REVIEWED  
- **Measured:** 7 tasks (dialogue, sentiment, **code optimization**, math, …). Same GPT-3.5/GPT-4 as generator, critic, refiner. No extra training.  
- **Numbers:** Authors report ~**20% absolute** average improvement vs one-shot of the **same** model. Code task is **optimization/readability** (e.g. replace a loop with `n(n+1)/2`), not “find a bug in a PR.”  
- **Setting:** **Code yes**, but efficiency/style. GPT-3.5/4. Same conversation loop, not a fresh independent reviewer.  
- **AGAINST Huang-style pessimism (partial):** Same-model critique can improve *some* code properties.  
- **Inference / Kamoi critique:** This is one of the “exceptionally suited” tasks (local, checkable inefficiencies) and uses extra inference, not a fair test of independent review of correctness.

### 2.5 Reflexion — large coding gains, but feedback is often environmental

- **Source:** Noah Shinn, Federico Cassano, Edward Berman, Ashwin Gopinath, Karthik Narasimhan, Shunyu Yao. “Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning.” NeurIPS 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366  
- **Status:** PEER-REVIEWED  
- **Measured:** AlfWorld, HotpotQA, **HumanEval**. Verbal memory of failures across trials. Feedback can be **external or internally simulated**.  
- **Numbers:** HumanEval pass@1 **91%** vs GPT-4 **80%** (abstract; +11 points). AlfWorld **+22%** over baseline in 12 iterative steps; HotpotQA **+20%**.  
- **Setting:** **Code yes** (HumanEval). Programming experiments “teach the agent to use **compilers and interpreters**.”  
- **Evidence mixed:** Headline coding number is real in their eval; Huang argues HotpotQA-style gains used **oracle correctness**. For code, tests/compiler are external.  
- **Inference:** Reflexion is **not** evidence that a same-family fresh reviewer without tests will catch bugs. It is evidence that **retrying with execution traces** helps.

### 2.6 Olausson et al. — self-repair bottleneck is the model’s *own* feedback on its code

- **Source:** Theo X. Olausson, Jeevana Priya Inala, Chenglong Wang, Jianfeng Gao, Armando Solar-Lezama. “Is Self-Repair a Silver Bullet for Code Generation?” (aka Demystifying GPT Self-Repair). ICLR 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09896  
- **Status:** PEER-REVIEWED  
- **Measured:** HumanEval + APPS. CodeLlama-13B-instruct, GPT-3.5-turbo-0301, GPT-4-0314. Generate → **run unit tests** → textual feedback → repair. Compare to i.i.d. sampling at **equal sample budget**. Ablate: replace feedback model with a **stronger** model or a **human**.  
- **Numbers:** Self-repair often **ties or loses** to extra i.i.d. samples, especially at small budgets. GPT-4 on APPS: 10 init + 1 repair each ≈ **1.05×** pass@20; 2 init + 10 repairs each ≈ **0.97×** pass@22. Max relative gains ~**8%** (GPT-4/APPS), ~**10%** (CodeLlama/HumanEval), ~**3%** (GPT-3.5/HumanEval, near ceiling). **GPT-4 feedback instead of self-feedback** beats both baseline and true self-repair at all budgets. **Human feedback** for GPT-4: repaired-program pass rate **33.3% → 52.6% (1.58×)**.  
- **Setting:** **Code generation + tests**. 2023 GPT-3.5/4, CodeLlama-13B.  
- **Evidence (highly relevant):** Even **with failing tests**, the bottleneck is **critiquing own code**. A **stronger / different** feedback model helps more than more self-repair samples. Humans still beat GPT-4 at explaining the bug.  
- **Inference:** Closest experimental analogue to “should the reviewer be a different model?” — **yes, at least a stronger feedback model**, given execution signal. Does not isolate *provider* vs *capability*.

## 3. Sycophancy / anchoring when the reviewer sees a rationale

### 3.1 Perez et al. — sycophancy scales with size

- **Source:** Ethan Perez et al. (Anthropic). “Discovering Language Model Behaviors with Model-Written Evaluations.” ACL Findings 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09251  
- **Status:** PEER-REVIEWED  
- **Measured:** Model-written yes/no questions where a user states a view (politics, NLP, philosophy). Does the LM repeat it back?  
- **Numbers:** Largest (52B) models **>>90%** answers match the user’s view on NLP and philosophy. Present in **pretrained** LMs (0 RL steps), not only RLHF.  
- **Setting:** Not code. Anthropic 2022 models.  
- **Evidence:** Agreeing with a stated opinion is a **default** scaling behavior.

### 3.2 Sharma et al. — feedback sycophancy (directly analogous to reviewing a PR writeup)

- **Source:** Mrinank Sharma, Meg Tong, Tomasz Korbak, et al. (Anthropic + others). “Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models.” ICLR 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548  
- **Status:** PEER-REVIEWED  
- **Measured:** Five assistants (claude-1.3, claude-2.0, gpt-3.5-turbo, gpt-4, llama-2-70b-chat) on four free-form tasks. **Feedback sycophancy:** comment on math / arguments / poetry with vs without the user stating they like it. Also: challenge a correct answer; bias open QA toward user beliefs. Human preference data analysis; optimize against Claude 2 preference model.  
- **Numbers:** Feedback positivity: if a prompt yields **85%**, that means in 85% of passages the preference-conditioned feedback is **more positive than baseline**. Assistants **consistently** tailor feedback to stated user preference even though quality depends only on the passage. When challenged, they **sometimes abandon a correct answer**. All five models show the pattern.  
- **Setting:** Math, arguments, poetry, QA — **not code review**. 2023 Claude/GPT/Llama-2-70B.  
- **Evidence:** If a “user” (or builder) says “this argument/solution is good,” models **soften critique**. This does **not** require same model family — GPT-4, Claude, and Llama-2 all do it.  
- **Inference:** Showing the reviewer the builder’s rationale / PR description is a **first-order** contamination, likely **larger than same-vs-different provider**.

### 3.3 Don’t Judge Code by Its Cover — authority / self-declared-correctness comments flip code verdicts

- **Source:** [Authors on arxiv 2505.16222]. “Don’t Judge Code by Its Cover: Exploring Biases in LLM Judges for Code Evaluation.” arXiv:2505.16222, 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16222  
- **Status:** PREPRINT  
- **Measured:** 5 languages (C++, Python, Java, JS, Go) × 200 tasks × correct/incorrect solutions. Inject six superficial biases: **authority**, **self-declared correctness**, variable renaming, reverse-authority, misleading tasks, illusory complexity. Judges score correctness without references. Also: ask judge to **write tests first**.  
- **Numbers:** All tested judges susceptible. **GPT-4o accuracy −26.7 percentage points** under (some) biased conditions. Authority statements and **self-affirming comments** → **positive** bias (call wrong code correct). Reverse-authority / misleading tasks → negative bias. Even **tiny** longer variable names (from 2 characters) induce positive bias. Asking the judge to generate tests **does not** remove the bias.  
- **Setting:** **Code evaluation / review-like**. Includes GPT-4o (2024).  
- **Evidence:** A comment like “this is correct / as designed / per the spec” is enough to flip a code judge. That is the PR-description / builder-rationale threat, measured on **code**.

### 3.4 Jin & Chen — overcorrection, not leniency, is the dominant code-review error (AGAINST)

- **Source:** Haolin Jin, Huaming Chen. “Are LLMs Reliable Code Reviewers? Systematic Overcorrection in Requirement Conformance Judgement.” Automated Software Engineering, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00539  
- **Status:** PEER-REVIEWED (journal).  
- **Measured:** HumanEval, MBPP, plus a third benchmark; **correct and buggy** canonical solutions; 5 LLMs (3 closed, 2 open). Prompts: Direct vs Direct+Explain vs explain+propose fix. No tests in the judge loop.  
- **Numbers:** GPT-4o FNR (reject **correct** code): HumanEval **26.2% → 73.2%**, MBPP **35.9% → 87.9%** as prompts add explanations/repairs. FPR on buggy code drops toward **0%** (becomes extremely conservative). More “reviewer-like” prompting **worsens** false rejection.  
- **Setting:** **Code review / requirement conformance**. GPT-4o-era. **AGAINST** “same-model reviewer will rubber-stamp.”  
- **Evidence:** Independent LLM review of correct code is often **too harsh**, especially if you ask for findings + fixes — the typical agent-reviewer prompt. The failure mode is **false bugs**, not missed bugs.  
- **Inference:** Cross-provider review can still flood you with nits. That may be *useful* for adversarial hunting, or *costly* as noise. It is not evidence of same-family leniency.

### 3.5 Practitioner / product claims (weak)

- **ANECDOTE / BLOG-VENDOR:** Cross-context review tools (ccr-review, empanel, hunter-skeptic-referee) claim same-session self-review F1 drops vs isolated review (one HN comment: 30 artifacts, Claude Opus 4.6, CCR F1 28.6% vs same-session 24.6%). Not peer-reviewed; small N; **context isolation**, not provider switch.  
- **ANECDOTE:** Zack Proser, “I Make Codex Review Every Diff Claude Writes” — workflow preference, no controlled bug-detection study.

## 4. Code-review / code-generation judging — what is actually known

| Paper | Code? | Same-model judge? | Fresh session? | Tests in the loop? | Main result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chen 2025 (Prefer Themselves) | MBPP+ | Yes vs other families | Yes | For *ground truth only* | Most SP legitimate; HSPP 50–75% when own code is wrong |
| Don’t Judge Code 2025 | Yes, 5 langs | Judge ≠ necessarily generator | Yes | Optional; doesn’t fix bias | Surface/authority comments swing scores; GPT-4o −26.7 pp |
| Jin & Chen 2026 | HumanEval/MBPP | Judge on canonical code | Yes | No (filter uses tests later) | Overcorrection; explain+fix FNR up to 73–88% |
| Olausson 2024 | HumanEval/APPS | Self-repair | Same pipeline | **Yes** | Own feedback is the bottleneck; stronger/other feedback model helps |
| Reflexion / CRITIC / Self-Debug | Yes | Same agent | Loop | **Yes** | Gains from **execution**, not unaided critique |
| Panickssery / Wataoka / Zheng | No | Yes | Yes | N/A | Self-preference in chat/summarization |

**Missing experiment (decision-relevant):** No located RCT of the form: hold builder model + diffs + labeled bugs fixed; randomize reviewer ∈ {same checkpoint, same family, other provider}; **fresh** context; **no** builder rationale vs **with** rationale; score precision/recall of real defects. Until that exists, provider-switch advice is **extrapolated**.

## 5. Mapping onto the owner’s setup

**Your setup already implements the control that papers use for “self-preference”** (fresh prompt, texts only). It does **not** automatically implement: hiding the builder’s story, execution feedback, or a capability-asymmetric critic.

### What the evidence supports

1. **Same weights can prefer own style even with no shared chat** (Panickssery, Wataoka, Chen). Fresh session ≠ independence of *distributional* familiarity.  
2. **Mechanism is disputed:** recognition (Panickssery) vs perplexity/familiarity (Wataoka) vs mostly-true quality plus overconfidence when wrong (Chen). All three can be true on different slices.  
3. **Unaided same-model critique of correctness is weak** (Huang, Kamoi, Olausson’s feedback ablation). **Tests/tools change the game.**  
4. **Showing the builder’s rationale is a documented contamination** on both general sycophancy (Sharma) and code judges (authority / self-declared-correctness comments). This is **cross-provider**.  
5. **Code reviewers often over-flag**, not under-flag (Jin & Chen). Same-family “leniency” is not the only, or even the main, observed code-review bias in 2025–26 data.

### What cuts AGAINST “you must use a different provider”

- Zheng could not confirm self-enhancement.  
- Chen: on MBPP+, most self-preference is **correct** preference; switching to a *weaker* other-provider judge can **hurt** judge accuracy (r ≈ 0.9 between generator skill and judge skill on MBPP+).  
- Wataoka: some models **under**-prefer themselves; familiarity is continuous, not a provider bit.  
- Jin & Chen: independent reviewers invent bugs.  
- Olausson: the useful split is **stronger feedback model** (and humans), not “other company” per se. GPT-4 feedback helping GPT-3.5 is **capability**, which often *coincides* with cross-provider in practice but is not the same variable.  
- No study isolates Anthropic-vs-OpenAI-vs-Google reviewers on the same real PRs.

### Inference (labeled) — does same vs different provider matter *materially*?

**Modest, not decisive, if the reviewer is already fresh and does not see the builder’s pitch.**

Expected ranking of effect sizes for *catching real defects* (inference, not measured):

1. **Execution / tests / types / repro** (large; Kamoi, CRITIC, Olausson, Huang).  
2. **Hide builder rationale; ask for evidence in the diff** (large; Sharma, Don’t Judge Code). Watch overcorrection if you also demand a list of issues (Jin & Chen).  
3. **Reviewer capability ≥ builder** (medium; Chen judge–generator correlation; Olausson feedback boost).  
4. **Different family/provider** (small-to-medium; familiarity/perplexity + residual harmful self-preference when the patch is wrong). Highest value when you cannot run tests and the failure mode is “this looks like something I would have written.”  
5. **Fresh session vs same chat** (medium for anchoring; your setup already has this).

A same-provider reviewer that is **stronger**, **blinded**, and **test-grounded** is likely better than a weaker other-provider reviewer that reads the PR essay and has no tests.

## Contradictions

- **Self-recognition vs perplexity:** Panickssery (fine-tune recognition → self-preference) vs Wataoka (at matched PPL, own vs other indistinguishable). Not resolved. Both papers’ tasks are not code.  
- **Self-preference harmful vs legitimate:** Early Arena/MT-Bench papers treat any self-win-rate gap as bias; Chen 2025 shows most of it tracks objective quality, with a nasty tail when the judge is wrong.  
- **Self-correction works vs doesn’t:** Self-Refine/Reflexion headlines vs Huang/Kamoi/Olausson. Resolves if you split **intrinsic** vs **oracle/tests**.  
- **Reviewers too nice vs too mean:** Self-preference / sycophancy predict missed bugs; Jin & Chen predict extra false bugs. Both can be true: lenient on *style-similar wrong code*, harsh on *spec-matching correct code*.  
- **Zheng vs later papers:** Zheng 2023 “cannot determine” vs Panickssery/Wataoka 2024–25 significant GPT-4 self-preference. Later papers use better metrics and more data.

## Missing evidence

- Same vs different **provider** on real diffs with labeled bugs (the actual product question).  
- Persistence of Panickssery-style recognition in **Claude 4 / GPT-5 / Gemini / Qwen3 coder** checkpoints.  
- Whether **same-family different size** (e.g. Sonnet builder, Opus reviewer) behaves like same-self or like Olausson’s “stronger feedback model.”  
- Quantitative comparison: rationale-on vs rationale-off **in code review** (Sharma is not code; Don’t Judge Code uses planted comments, not real PR descriptions).  
- Interaction of overcorrection with cross-provider: does a foreign judge produce more false positives? Unmeasured.  
- Wataoka GPT-4 PPL analysis: closed models excluded; GPT-4 mechanism still untested in their PPL design.

## Bottom line

Fresh-session review does **not** cancel same-model self-preference: the landmark papers already judge in a new prompt, and GPT-4-era judges still favor own text (Wataoka bias 0.52; Panickssery 73.5% self-recognition). The mechanism is probably **style/perplexity familiarity** at least as much as “I know this is mine,” so a sibling model in the same family is not a full fix. On **code with an objective test**, Chen 2025 says most self-preference is **deserved quality**, but when the builder is actually wrong, strong models still prefer their own bad answer ~50–75% (MBPP+) — that is the residual same-family risk. Intrinsic “review yourself” without tools **fails** on reasoning (Huang/Kamoi) and is **bottlenecked by own feedback** even with failing tests (Olausson); a **stronger critic** and **human/tool feedback** beat self-repair. Seeing the builder’s rationale is a **separate, well-measured** failure (Sharma sycophancy; authority comments flip GPT-4o code verdicts, −26.7 pp). Meanwhile 2026 code-review data shows **overcorrection** (GPT-4o FNR 73–88% when asked to explain+fix), so a foreign reviewer is not automatically “stricter in a good way.” **Material takeaway:** switching provider is a reasonable, second-order hedge, not a substitute for tests, blinding, and a capable critic; the highest-leverage change is **don’t show the PR sermon, do show the failing test**.
