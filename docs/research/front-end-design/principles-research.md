# Research: cross-surface UI and visual design fundamentals

## Summary
Ranked by priority: understandable and operable first, then visual organization and polish. Tests are researcher operationalizations, not claims that a screenshot proves usability; WCAG is a web standard and its CSS-pixel values are not universal terminal/native units. Evidence labels distinguish standards, established perceptual/experimental models, and practitioner convention.

## Near-universal principles

1. **Make actions discoverable.** Why: a signifier communicates an action; the affordance is the possibility of acting, not the cue itself (Norman, practitioner theory). Test: identify every primary action in a screenshot; verify label/control shape and that keyboard activation works. [Norman](https://jnd.org/signifiers-not-affordances/).
2. **Show system status and result.** Why: prompt feedback reduces uncertainty about what happened (usability heuristic, not a guaranteed quantitative effect). Test: invoke save, submit, and a long operation; observe progress/result and accessible announcement. [Nielsen](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/).
3. **Prevent and recover from mistakes.** Why: error prevention, understandable diagnosis, undo/cancel, and actionable recovery reduce costly dead ends (heuristic). Test: provoke invalid input and a failure; check field-specific messages, retained input, and available recovery. [Nielsen](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/).
4. **Ensure keyboard operation and visible focus.** Why: people use different input and perception modes (WCAG standard). Test: tab through all controls, operate them, check focus visible and not obscured; inspect semantic names. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#keyboard-accessible), [focus visible](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-visible.html), [GNOME accessibility](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/guidelines/accessibility.html).
5. **Make content and state distinguishable without color alone.** Why: color perception varies and information may be monochrome (WCAG standard). Test: inspect grayscale and add text, shape, icon or pattern to error/success/selection; check color pairs in both themes. [WCAG use of color](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#use-of-color).
6. **Maintain contrast for text and necessary UI boundaries.** Why: insufficient luminance contrast impedes perception (WCAG standard). Test computed foreground/background: normal text ≥4.5:1, large ≥3:1, required component/state and graphical information ≥3:1 against adjacent colors, subject to exceptions; test actual composed colors in both themes. These are WCAG AA web criteria, not a claim of universal conformance. [Text](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [non-text](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html).
7. **Group related things, separate unrelated things.** Why: Gestalt proximity and common region bias grouping; similarity implies relationship, continuity draws the eye along aligned paths, and figure/ground distinguishes content from background (perceptual principles; exact effects vary with context). Test: within-group gaps smaller than between-group gaps; related controls share visual treatment; containers do not accidentally group unrelated controls; follow alignment and foreground separation in a screenshot. [NN/g visual design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/), [proximity](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-proximity/), [common region](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/common-region/), [similarity](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-similarity/).
8. **Make importance visible through hierarchy.** Why: contrast in size, weight, position, and color differentiates levels of importance (practitioner principle grounded in visual perception). Test: in a screenshot name the task, primary action, section headings and secondary details at a glance; check competing items do not have identical emphasis. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/).
9. **Use consistent alignment and relationships.** Why: aligned edges and recurring spatial relationships help organize the page (practitioner guidance). Test: overlay vertical/horizontal guides, check labels and controls share deliberate anchors, and flag unexplained one-off gaps; allow exceptions for content. [Fluent layout](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/layout).
10. **Use legible typography with a small hierarchy.** Why: size, weight and measure distinguish structures while readable letterforms and spacing support reading (standards and practitioner convention). Test: check body type at intended viewing size, distinguish heading levels without depending solely on color, and apply WCAG text-spacing overrides without clipping; 1.5 line-height is an override requirement, not a mandatory default. [W3C text spacing](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/text-spacing.html), [GNOME typography](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/guidelines/typography.html).
11. **Size and locate targets for the input method.** Why: Fitts's law experimentally models aimed movement time as a function of target width and distance, not visual button area alone. Test: inspect real hitboxes, spacing and frequent-action reach; on web meet WCAG AA 24×24 CSS px or a specified exception, including its spacing test. Native touch recommendations can be larger; TUI keyboard focus is a different interaction. [Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/fittss-law/), [WCAG target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html).
12. **Make choices manageable without hiding essential work.** Why: Hick–Hyman experiments connect choice reaction time with stimulus uncertainty in simple controlled tasks; this is not proof that fewer visible menu entries always improve a complex workflow. Test: review a representative task for confusing choices and measure completion and errors before/after grouping. [Research on HCI limitations](https://perso.telecom-paristech.fr/rioul/publis/202001liugoririoulbeaudouinlafonguiard.pdf), [Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/).
13. **Honor task and information density.** Why: irrelevant visual elements compete with useful information, but labels, ticks and grouping may enable interpretation (practitioner heuristic with mixed empirical support). Test: remove a decorative element, then check whether comprehension or comparison suffers; verify task-critical data remain visible. Tufte's data-ink is a question, not a universal mandate to minimize every pixel. [Graph experiment, abstract access restricted](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/001872089403600405), [Nielsen minimalist design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/).
14. **Adapt layout to available space and user settings.** Why: a fixed composition fails when window, zoom, orientation or text size changes (platform guidance and WCAG standard). Test narrow and wide windows, zoom/large text and overflow; web reflow at 320 CSS px applies with exceptions such as content requiring two-dimensional layout. Terminal: test widths and heights in cells. [WCAG reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html), [GNOME](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/guidelines/adaptive.html), [Android](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/layout-and-content/adapt-layout).
15. **Design every interaction state.** Why: users need to understand what can happen, what is happening and what happened (Nielsen heuristic; state checklist is researcher synthesis). Test actual empty, loading, success, error, disabled, focus and hover states; focus and instructions must not depend on hover, and disabled actions should have a discoverable reason when needed. [Nielsen](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/), [WCAG keyboard](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#keyboard).
16. **Use motion only when useful, and support reduced motion.** Why: motion can explain change but can trigger vestibular symptoms (standards and platform guidance). Test with reduced-motion preference on; nonessential interaction-triggered motion can be disabled under WCAG 2.3.3 AAA, and state change remains understandable without animation. [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html), [Windows motion](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/motion/).
17. **Use consistent interaction patterns and semantic design tokens.** Why: consistency supports recognition; shared named roles make theme and style changes coherent (heuristic and practitioner engineering convention). Test: compare equivalent controls and inspect tokens for surface, text, action, status, spacing, type and focus across light/dark themes; tokens do not prove adequate contrast. [Nielsen](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/), [Fluent tokens](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/design-tokens).

## Useful conventions, not universal laws

18. **Use a spacing scale and grid as defaults, not a straitjacket.** Why: a limited vocabulary helps consistency and alignment (practitioner convention; no universal base unit). Test: audit repeated gaps against project tokens; allow documented optical/content exceptions. Fluent uses a 4-pixel base, not a rule for terminals or all platforms. [Fluent layout](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/layout).
19. **Choose a type scale and reading measure appropriate to the task.** Why: recurring sizes communicate levels and constrained measure can support comfortable long-form reading (practitioner convention; no single optimal measure). Test: inspect consistent type roles; verify long paragraphs are not excessively wide and text survives resizing; for web the separate WCAG AAA visual-presentation criterion specifies 80 characters, 40 for CJK, among other conditions. Relative line height need not be identical across type sizes. [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/visual-presentation.html), [Refactoring UI](https://refactoringui.com/previews/line-height-is-proportional).
20. **Restrict color roles, not creative identity.** Why: a small set of semantic roles supports predictable distinctions (practitioner convention); no evidence establishes one mandatory palette or a universal number of colors. Test: identify purpose of each recurring color and verify each role pair in both themes. [Fluent tokens](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/design-tokens), [WCAG color](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#use-of-color).
21. **Honor the host platform.** Why: familiar conventions reduce relearning, but platform specifics differ (heuristic and platform convention). Test: compare navigation, menus, shortcuts, focus and accessibility to the relevant host; don't impose touch patterns on a keyboard-first terminal. The shared broad themes verified here are adaptability, accessibility, and coherent experience, not identical metrics or visuals. [GNOME](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/guidelines/adaptive.html), [Windows](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/design-principles), [Android](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/layout-and-content/adapt-layout), [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/).

## Heuristic cross-check
[Nielsen's ten](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/): visibility of system status; match with real world; user control and freedom; consistency and standards; error prevention; recognition rather than recall; flexibility and efficiency; aesthetic and minimalist design; recognize, diagnose and recover from errors; help and documentation. These are inspection heuristics, not a substitute for task testing. Test: check each explicitly against a representative workflow, including expert shortcuts and discoverable help.

## Contradictions and limits
- More whitespace and minimalism can improve grouping, but a dense expert dashboard or terminal can benefit from visible information; evaluate task performance rather than enforcing sparse landing-page aesthetics. [Fluent spacing](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/layout), [Nielsen efficiency](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/).
- Hick's simplified fewer-choices advice conflicts with its limited experimental scope; hiding choices can add steps. Treat this latter risk as researcher inference and test it. [HCI research](https://perso.telecom-paristech.fr/rioul/publis/202001liugoririoulbeaudouinlafonguiard.pdf).
- Rams advocates as little design as possible, while other needs require cues, status and accessibility; do not interpret this as removing useful information. [Rams via Vitsœ](https://www.vitsoe.com/us/about/good-design), [Nielsen](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/).
- WCAG focus-visible is AA; the particular area and focused/unfocused 3:1 contrast-change criterion in focus appearance is AAA. Non-text contrast AA can separately apply to focus indicators. [W3C focus](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-appearance.html).
- Source-check retrieved passages but automated semantic assessment was unavailable; the W3C and research documents were inspected directly. Material 3 and Apple HIG pages were JavaScript-rendered and could not be fetched; their detailed agreement is **unverified**, so Android and Windows/GNOME are used for direct evidence and no Material/Apple numeric rule is asserted. Graph study full text returned 403; specific empirical effect sizes are **unverified**.

## What this means for the skill
Teach agents to identify surface, task, input modes and constraints first; select a compatible visual system; explicitly design states; inspect screenshots for grouping/hierarchy while testing interactions, semantics and contrast in code or the running UI. Distinguish standards from design heuristics, use platform-specific metrics only where applicable, and validate consequential design decisions with actual users.
